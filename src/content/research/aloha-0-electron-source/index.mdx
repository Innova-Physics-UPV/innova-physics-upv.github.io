Every accelerator starts with a source. ALOHA-0 is ours: a thermionic triode that pulls electrons off a hot tungsten cathode and shapes them into a beam before anything accelerates them.

## Three electrodes

The cathode is heated until it emits. The Wehnelt cylinder around it, held slightly negative, pushes the emission into a converging beam. The anode, 30&nbsp;kV above the cathode, pulls the electrons through its aperture.

## Space charge

A source cannot draw as much current as it likes: the electrons already in the gap push back on the ones leaving the cathode. For a planar gap of width $d$ across a voltage $V$, the current density is capped by the Child–Langmuir law,

$$
J = \frac{4\varepsilon_0}{9}\sqrt{\frac{2e}{m_e}}\,\frac{V^{3/2}}{d^2},
$$

so the current a source can carry grows as $V^{3/2}$. The ratio $P = I/V^{3/2}$, the perveance, describes the source independently of the voltage it runs at.

## What is measured, and what is not

Every number on this page comes from COMSOL. None is measured yet: the source is designed and simulated, and the vacuum bench to test it is what we build next.
